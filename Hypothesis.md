# Buyer Profile & Hypotheses: Jennifer Montgomery

## Buyer Profile

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Jennifer Montgomery |
| Role | Buyer |
| Budget | High |
| Motivation | Wants to show off / status property |
| Timing | Within 1 month |
| Key preferences | Waterfront, renovated, high grade |
| Investment horizon | Resell within 1 year |

Jennifer is a high-budget buyer on a short timeline who wants a statement property: waterfront, recently renovated, and high-grade construction. Because she plans to resell within a year, her purchase decision needs to balance prestige with resale value, which makes price drivers like location, waterfront status, and renovation particularly relevant to her search.

## Hypotheses

### Hypothesis 1: Location and grade interact to drive top-tier prices

In more expensive zip codes, houses with higher grades are more likely to fall into the top-price segment.

*Relevance to Jennifer:* Since she wants a property that shows off her status, understanding which zip codes reward high construction/design grade with the biggest price premium helps target her search to areas where a high grade will translate into maximum perceived value.

### Hypothesis 2: Waterfront properties command a price premium

Waterfront houses tend to be generally more expensive than non-waterfront houses.

*Relevance to Jennifer:* Waterfront is a non-negotiable preference for her. Confirming the size of the price premium helps set realistic budget expectations and supports resale value analysis, since waterfront status is a durable, high-demand feature.

### Hypothesis 3: Recent renovations boost sale price

Recently renovated houses tend to sell for higher prices than non-renovated houses with similar characteristics.

*Relevance to Jennifer:* She specifically wants a renovated home. Testing this hypothesis (controlling for comparable characteristics) clarifies how much of a premium renovation adds, which matters both for her purchase price and for her 1-year resale strategy.
